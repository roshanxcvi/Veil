The problem

You're the dataset, and nobody told you.

Open a typical news or shopping page and — before you've read a word — it may already be recording your every move, fingerprinting your device, and streaming your behavior into systems that train AI. You agreed to none of it in a way you'd recognize as agreement.

01 Your sessions get recorded

Replay tools capture every mouse move, scroll, and sometimes keystroke — and have leaked passwords, card numbers, and medical info typed into forms.

02 Fingerprinting beats incognito

A unique signature built from your GPU, canvas, and fonts re-identifies you even when cookies are cleared and you're in private mode.

03 Your behavior is training data

Clickstreams, dwell time, and chat transcripts increasingly flow into recommendation, ranking, and generative-AI pipelines.

04 Brokers resell who you are

Identity graphs assembled from these signals are sold to advertisers, insurers, and employers with almost no visibility to you.

Why it's different

Not another ad blocker. A receipt.

Most blockers show you a number. Veil shows you which company tried to collect your data, what they were after, and why it matters — and only targets the behavioral / AI data layer.

What Veil won't pretend to do.

It can't block server-side AI crawlers.

Bots like GPTBot and ClaudeBot hit a website's server directly and never touch your browser — that's the site operator's job, via robots.txt or CDN rules.

It's not a general ad blocker.

It won't strip every banner or skip video ads. Pair it with uBlock Origin if that's what you want. Veil's job is the behavioral / AI data layer, explained.

Determined fingerprinters can detect the wrappers.

Client-side detection is inherently visible to aggressive scripts. Veil prioritizes not breaking the web over being invisible.

Install

Running in under a minute.

Veil isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet — install it manually as an unpacked extension.

Download

Grab the repo from GitHub (Code → Download ZIP) and unzip it.

Open extensions

Go to chrome://extensions in Chrome.

Developer mode

Toggle Developer mode on, top-right.

Load unpacked

Click Load unpacked and pick the folder with manifest.json. Pin the ▲ icon.
